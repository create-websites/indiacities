Ajanta & Ellora Caves

Ancient hand-cut masterpieces of Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain rock architecture.

Historical Marvels

The Ajanta and Ellora caves, known for their hand-carved rock structures, are among India's most iconic destinations. They were discovered in 1819 by a British Officer. John Smith While on a hunting expedition, one cannot help but marvel at the ancient artistic brilliance displayed in these caves.

Ajanta: 29 Caves (200BC - 650AD)
Ellora: 34 Caves (350AD - 700AD)
Near Aurangabad, Maharashtra
Ajanta: UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Ajanta Caves

Ajanta is home to 30 caves, comprising of 5 prayer halls (Chaityas) and 25 monasteries (Viharas), all carved into a hors

Key Artistic Highlights

  • Cave 1: Features the famous Bodhisattva Padmapani and Vajrapani paintings.
  • Cave 2: Famous for the painting of the 'Thousand Buddhas' and the princess swinging.
  • Cave 11: Illustrates the shift from Hinayana to Mahayana worship through the addition of Buddha images to a stupa.
  • Cave 17: Contains strict interior arrangements and the Shad-Danta Jataka paintings.

The Ellora Caves

Known locally as 'Veru Lani', Ellora is the biggest monolithic excavation globally.

The Kailasa Temple (Cave 16)

The main attraction of Ellora, initiated by Krishna I from the Rashtrakutha dynasty, showcases a magnificent Dravidian art accomplishment with a Nandi Mandap and a central Shiva temple.

The Dashavatara (Cave 15)

Includes a two-story temple showcasing panels depicting the ten avatars of Vishnu, including the defeat of Hiranyakashipu.

Carpenter's Cave (Cave 10)

The renowned Buddhist cave, Vishwakarma Cave, is home to a 15-foot seated Buddha statue.

River Goddesses (Cave 21)

Identified as Ramesvara cave, characterized by the depiction of goddesses Ganga and Yamuna at the entrance.
