Jaisalmer: The Golden City

An impressive oasis in the midst of India's Thar Desert.

Desert Heritage

Named after its founder, Rao Jaisal YadavJaisalmer, often referred to as the 'Golden City', gets its name from the yellow sand that gives a golden hue to the town and its surroundings. Once a princely state, it is located in the heart of the desert. Thar Desert[cite: 580, 581].

Jaisalmer Fort

Constructed in 1156 by Jaisal, the Bhati ruler, on Trikuta hill, the fort is renowned as the 'Sonar Kella' (Golden Fort). The walls, resembling the color of lions, transform into a honey-gold shade at sunset.

Main Attractions:

  • Rajmahal: The royal palace[cite: 585].
  • Temples: Includes Laxminath and various Jain temples[cite: 585].

Architectural Wonders: The Havelis

Patwo ki Haveli

The city's biggest and most charming haveli was constructed by merchant Guman Chand Patwa and his sons. It took 50 years to finish this five-story complex.

Salam Singh ki Haveli

Constructed in 1815 under the leadership of the Prime Minister at that time, this structure showcases arched rooftops adorned with brackets resembling peacocks[cite: 587,

Nathmalji ki Haveli

Elaborately adorned with birds, elephants, flowers, and distinctive contemporary carvings such as a steam engine and bicycle [source: 593].

Shreenath ji ki Haveli

A 15th-century haveli, currently home to the descendants of the original Vyas family, also serving as a partially operational hotel[cite: 594, 595].

Jain Heritage

The city boasts beautiful Jain temples dedicated to Lord Sai Nath (16th Tirthankar) and Lord Parshvanath (23rd Tirthankar)[cite: 596].

Amarsagar, Lodavra, Pokharan, and Brahmsar are some of the other pilgrimage centers
