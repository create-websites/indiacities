Dandeli Wildlife Sanctuary

Home of the mysterious Black Panther and untamed river adventures.

Quick Facts

Location: Uttara Kannada, Karnataka
Area: 5729.07 sq. km
Best Time: October to March
Timings: 6:00 AM – 6:00 PM
Highlights: Black Panther, Rafting, Trekking

Overview

Dandeli Wildlife Sanctuary is the second largest sanctuary in Karnataka, known for its extensive moist deciduous and semi-evergreen forests, and is particularly renowned for being the habitat of the elusive. Black Panther, immortalized as Bagheera in The Jungle Book.

The Mysterious Panther

Although spotting these melanistic leopards is a matter of chance, official records show that there are eight of them in the sanctuary. Their black fur gives them a major edge in hunting within the quiet forests.

The River Kali along with its tributaries, Kaneri and Nagajhar, wind their way through the park, serving as a vital source of life for a variety of wildlife and a thrilling destination for adventure enthusiasts.

Wildlife & Adventure

The Inhabitants

In addition to the panther, you can encounter tigers, elephants, gaur (bison), sloth bears, and crocodiles in the forest. Wild dogs, jackals, giant flying squirrels, and different types of deer like barking, spotted, and mouse deer also inhabit the area.

Birdwatcher's Paradise

The sanctuary is a refuge for a variety of bird species, such as the Malabar pied hornbill, crested serpent eagle, magpie robin, and white-breasted kingfisher, but it is especially known for its large population of Hornbills.

Must-Visit Sightseeing

Kavala Caves (30 km)

The ancient limestone caves, once inhabited by prehistoric man, boast distinctive Shivling-like stalagmites and are accessed by a challenging trek.

Shiroli Peak (25 km)

The district's highest peak offers stunning vistas of the Western Ghats and great trekking options.

Syntheri Rocks

A towering 300-ft limestone cliff looms over the sanctuary, adorned with numerous beehives and enigmatic caves nestled at its foot.

Kulgi Nature Camp (14 km)

Includes a nature interpretation center and museum, with the main attraction being a preserved 13-foot king cobra.
