A TOWER BUILT BY DYNASTIES

The Minar stands as a testament to India's layered history, its construction mirroring the visions and styles of various rulers across three centuries.

1

Qutb-ud-din Aibak (c. 1199)

Established the base and raised the first level as a triumph pillar.

2

Iltutmish (c. 1220)

Added the next three storeys, completing the original four-storey structure.

3

Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1368)

Following lightning damage, restored and built the 4th and 5th floors with marble.

4

Later Repairs (1505 & 1828)

Rebuilt by Sikandar Lodi post-quake, later fixed by British Major Smith.

ANATOMY OF A MASTERPIECE

The Minar blends Central Asian styles with Indian artistry, creating distinctive architectural elements.

✨

FLUTED SHAFT

Angular and rounded columns alternate, casting a striking dance of light and shadow.

📜

CALLIGRAPHY BANDS

Delicate Quranic verses and historic accounts are etched in graceful Kufic and Naskh calligraphy.

🍯

MUQARNAS CORBELS

Intricate honeycomb-patterned brackets, iconic in Islamic design, uphold every jutting balcony.

A MODERN ICON

The Qutub Minar, a UNESCO World Heritage site, remains a popular tourist attraction and is actively preserved by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI).

SURGING VISITOR NUMBERS (2021-2024)

Tourism has surged post-pandemic, fueled by new attractions like a laser show, now ranking it as the #2 spot for international visitors.

CONSERVATION SPENDING (2021-24)

The ASI steadily funds efforts to maintain the site, tackling structural repairs, water damage, and visitor amenities.
