Regional traditions

How Dussehra is celebrated city by city, from Mysuru Dasara and Kullu to Delhi, Varanasi, Pune, Hyderabad and Chennai.

20 guides by Prashant Dhingra

  • Dussehra in Delhi: Ramlila, Effigy Burning and Community Celebrations Delhi celebrates Dussehra with some of India's biggest Ramlilas, at Ramlila Maidan, the Red Fort grounds and hundreds of neighborhood stages, ending with towering Ravana effigies burned at dusk.
  • Dussehra in Varanasi: Ramnagar Ramlila and Local Traditions Varanasi's Dussehra centers on the Ramnagar Ramlila, a month-long promenade performance across the town opposite the ghats, patronized by the Kashi royal family since the 19th century.
  • Dussehra in Ayodhya: Rama Traditions and Festival Celebrations In Ayodhya, revered as Rama's birthplace, Dussehra brings Ramlila, temple worship, processions and effigy burning along the Saryu.
  • Dussehra in Mysuru: Royal Procession, Palace Lights and Traditions Mysuru Dasara is Karnataka's state festival (Nadahabba): ten days of cultural events, with Mysore Palace lit by nearly 100,000 bulbs each evening.
  • Kullu Dussehra: History, Deities and Week-Long Celebrations Kullu Dussehra begins on Vijayadashami, as celebrations elsewhere end, and runs seven days at Dhalpur Maidan.
  • Dussehra in Kota: Fairs, Effigies and Rajasthan Traditions Kota's Dussehra Mela is one of Rajasthan's largest festival fairs: several weeks of trade stalls, rides, cultural programs and concerts on the city's Dussehra grounds.
  • Dussehra in Jaipur: Ramlila, Fairs and Local Customs Jaipur marks Dussehra with neighborhood Ramlilas, fairs and Ravana Dahan at several large grounds across the city.
  • Dussehra in Lucknow: Ramlila and Awadhi Traditions Lucknow's Dussehra centers on its Ramlilas, especially Aishbagh, which local tradition traces back several centuries, followed by effigy burning across the city.
  • Dussehra in Kanpur: Fairs, Ramlila and Community Celebrations Kanpur celebrates Dussehra with Ramlilas, melas and Ravana Dahan across the city.
  • Dussehra in Patna: Ramlila and Bihar Traditions Patna's biggest Dussehra event is the Ravana Vadh at Gandhi Maidan, with large effigies and crowds, alongside Ramlilas across the city.
  • Dussehra in Ranchi: Local Celebrations and Jharkhand Traditions In Ranchi, Dussehra combines large Durga Puja pandals with Ravana Dahan, the biggest traditionally at Morabadi Ground.
  • Dussehra in Mumbai: Community Events and Vijayadashami Traditions Mumbai's Dussehra follows nights of Navratri garba and dandiya.
  • Dussehra in Pune: Simollanghan and Maharashtrian Customs Pune keeps classic Maharashtrian Dasara customs: Shastra and Saraswati puja, marigold torans, Simollanghan (symbolically crossing the boundary) and exchanging Apta leaves with the words 'sona ghya, sonyasarkha raha' (take gold, stay like gold).
  • Dussehra in Nagpur: Shami Puja and Regional Traditions Nagpur observes Dussehra with Shami puja, Apta leaf exchange and Ravana Dahan.
  • Dussehra in Hyderabad: Bathukamma Connections and Local Celebrations In Hyderabad, Dasara follows Bathukamma, Telangana's nine-day flower festival, which ends with Saddula Bathukamma.
  • Dussehra in Bengaluru: Ayudha Puja and Karnataka Traditions In Bengaluru, Dasara means Bombe Habba (doll displays), Ayudha Puja for vehicles, tools and computers on Navami, and Vijayadashami beginnings for students and businesses.
  • Dussehra in Chennai: Golu, Saraswati Puja and Vijayadashami Chennai celebrates the season with Navaratri Golu, doll displays on stepped platforms that neighbors visit for sundal and gifts.
  • Dussehra in Kerala: Vidyarambham and Learning Traditions In Kerala, Vijayadashami is the day of Vidyarambham: young children write their first letters in rice or sand, guided by a teacher or elder.
  • Dussehra in Goa: Shakti and Local Temple Traditions Goa's Dussehra is temple-centered: many temples, especially around Ponda, hold palkhi (palanquin) processions and Navratri festivities for the goddess.
  • How Dussehra Traditions Differ Across North, South, East and West India North India stresses Ramlila and Ravana Dahan; the east celebrates Durga Puja, visarjan and Bijoya.
