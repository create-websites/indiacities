Kollam: The Ancient Emporium

A historic seaport located along the shores of Ashtamudi Lake, where trade and natural beauty converge.

Quick Facts

Area: 2579 sq. km
Altitude: Sea level
Best Time: August to March
Annual Rainfall: 278 cm
Climate: Tropical
Aliases: Desinganadu, Quilon

History & Heritage

Kollam has been known for its commercial significance dating back to the Phoenicians and Romans. Renowned traveler Ibn Batuta considered it one of the primary ports for Chinese trade. Throughout its history, Kollam has been a hub for international relations, welcoming Portuguese, Dutch, and British trading hubs through the ages.

The Malayalam era originated in the 9th century with the establishment of Kollam. During Velu Thampi Dalawa's reign, the town underwent modernization, establishing itself as a thriving hub for commerce and industry.

Major Attractions

Kollam Town

Constructed in the 9th Century by Syrian merchant Sapir Iso, this town is the epicenter of the global cashew industry, situated on the banks of the renowned Ashtamudi Lake and acting as the entry point to Kerala's backwaters.

Palaruvi Waterfalls

Known as 'Stream of Milk,' this stunning waterfall in Kerala is situated near Aryankavu on the NH 208. The ideal time to visit is between June and January.

Thenmala Eco-Tourism

The initial eco-tourism spot in India, enclosed by thick woodlands, includes rubber and tea estates, a dam area, and timber trade.

Oachira Pilgrim Center

A distinctive hub devoted to worshiping the 'Parabrahmam,' free of temple structures or idols. Known for the Oachirakkali festival and intricate screw pine mat handicrafts.

Spiritual & Forest Ranges

Achencoil

A sacred site nestled in a thick forest, home to the ancient Sastha temple. Renowned for its extravagant flower offerings (Pushpabhishekam), it is a popular pilgrimage destination in South India.

Kulathupuzha

A renowned forest range famous for its abundance of elephants and providing materials for paper production.

Mayyanad

Famous for its nine shrines and temples, the Subramanian temple at Umayanallor stands out, believed to have been consecrated by Shri Sankaracharya.

Travel Guide

By Air

Thiruvananthapuram Airport is the nearest airport to Kollam, situated around 72 km

By Rail

Kollam serves as a key railway hub with connections to major cities in Kerala and across India.

By Road

Easily accessible via the Kollam-Alappuzha National Highway and other key state roads.

Local Transport

City travel options include KSRTC buses, private taxis, and auto-rickshaws that are easily accessible.
