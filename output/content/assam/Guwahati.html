Guwahati: The Light of the East

The bustling gateway to the exotic north-eastern regions of India.

Altitude: 55 meters
Best Time: October to April

History & Overview

Once known as 'Pragiyotishpura'Formerly the capital of Kamrup kings, the city now plays a crucial role as a service hub for the oil and tea industries, including housing India's biggest tea auction market.

The city is a mosaic of historic Hindu temples, with the Kamakhya temple at the center of Assamese life and culture.

Major Attractions

Kamakhya Temple

Situated on Nilachal hill, this ancient shrine from the 10th century is considered the most sacred place for Shakti worship and Tantric practices globally.

Navagraha Temple

The ancient 'Temple of Nine Planets' is a hub of astrology, with lingams housed in its red beehive dome representing celestial bodies such as the Sun, Moon, and Mars.

Umananda Shiva Temple

Located on a tiny island in the Brahmaputra River, this serene shrine can be reached by motor boats and ferries departing from Umananda Ghat.

Vashistha Ashram

Sage Vashistha's residence, located 12 km from the station, is a verdant sanctuary where three streams - Lalita, Kanta, and Sandhya - meet.

Nature & Spirituality

Guwahati Zoo

Discover the extensive natural zoo in the nation, highlighting the variety of wildlife found in the North East.

Bhubaneshwari Temple

Perched atop Kamakhya, this temple offers a breathtaking aerial perspective of the city and the flowing river below.

Ugratara Temple

This city shrine is renowned for its traditional rituals and buffalo sacrifices, as well as its iconic golden idol.

Travel Info

By Air

The Guwahati international airport links the city to key Indian destinations and global hubs such as Bangkok.

By Rail

Assam's primary railhead provides direct connections to New Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai, and Bangalore.

By Road

A sturdy road network with frequent state transport buses linking all key tourist spots.
