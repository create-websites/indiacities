Manas Tiger Reserve

Located in the foothills of the Himalayas, this UNESCO World Heritage Site is nestled in a picturesque setting.

Reserve Profile

Location: 176 km from Guwahati, Assam
Area: 391 sq. kms (Core) / 2840 sq. kms (Reserve)
Best Time: October to April
Climate: Min 2°C, Max 32°C

Historical Background

Manas is situated on the Indo-Bhutan border, with adjacent wildlife habitats in Bhutan. Historically, it was used as hunting grounds by the Raja of Gauripur and the Cooch-Behar royal family.

The Manas Sanctuary Established in 1928, the region was subsequently designated as National Park in 1990. It remains the only Project Tiger site in Assam.

A Unique Biosphere

The Majestic Tiger

Kaziranga is renowned for its rhinos, whereas Manas is celebrated for its tigers. The riparian forests offer the ideal habitat for tigers, boasting a remarkable abundance of the species.

Rare & Endangered Species

Habitat for twenty endangered species from the IUCN Red Data Book, including the rare. Golden LangurThe Hispid Hare, Pigmy Hog, and rare glimpses of the Red Panda are sometimes spotted.

Ecosystem & Habitat

The park's terrain consists of roughly 45% grassland and 55% tree landThe Manas River forms the border between India and Bhutan and fosters a dynamic environment of erosion and sediment deposition, supporting exceptionally high productivity.

Forest Dwellers

  • Elephant & Rhino
  • Water Buffalo
  • Hoolock Gibbon
  • Capped Langur

Avian Diversity

  • Giant Hornbills
  • Scarlet Minivet
  • Brahminy Ducks
  • Pelicans & Herons

Plan Your Trip

By Air

The closest airport to the reserve is Guwahati International Airport, which is situated 176 km away.

By Rail

The nearest town and railhead is Barpeta Road, which is 41 km away from the park entrance.

By Road

Manas is easily accessible via a well-connected road network to major towns in Assam as well as other cities in India.

Conservation Note

Manas is a delicate ecosystem where visitors are urged to admire wildlife from afar and honor the endangered species' natural environment.
