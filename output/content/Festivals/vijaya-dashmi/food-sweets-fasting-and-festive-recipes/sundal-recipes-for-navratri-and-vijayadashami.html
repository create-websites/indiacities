Sundal Recipes for Navratri and Vijayadashami

Sundal is a quick stir-fry of boiled legumes (chickpeas, black chana, green gram or peanuts) with a mustard and curry-leaf tempering and fresh coconut.

By Prashant Dhingra

Read the guide Jump to FAQs

In short

Sundal is a quick stir-fry of boiled legumes (chickpeas, black chana, green gram or peanuts) with a mustard and curry-leaf tempering and fresh coconut. In Tamil Nadu it is offered each evening to Golu visitors through Navaratri and on Vijayadashami. A basic chickpea sundal recipe is below.

Cultural note: Names, stories, rituals and sequencing can differ by region, language, sampradaya, family tradition and local calendar. Where several interpretations exist, this guide presents them as traditions rather than claiming that one version is universally authoritative.

Chickpea sundal (serves 4)

Ingredients: 1 cup white or black chickpeas (soaked overnight), 1 tbsp coconut oil, 1 tsp mustard seeds, 1 tsp urad dal, 1 or 2 dried red chillies, a sprig of curry leaves, a pinch of asafoetida, salt, and 3 tbsp grated fresh coconut.

  1. Pressure-cook the soaked chickpeas with salt until soft but whole (4 to 5 whistles). Drain.
  2. Heat the oil. Add mustard seeds; when they splutter, add urad dal, red chillies, curry leaves and asafoetida.
  3. Add the chickpeas and toss for 2 minutes. Turn off the heat and mix in the coconut.

Variations: use green gram, peanuts, rajma or sweet corn, add grated raw mango, or make sweet sundal with jaggery and coconut.

Dussehra food is regional

There is no single pan-Indian Dussehra menu. Festival foods reflect region, season, family tradition and whether a household has been fasting during Navratri. Depending on the community, the transition to Dussehra may involve a celebratory meal, prasad, sweets or regional dishes associated with Vijayadashami.

Build a menu around your family's traditions first, then add dishes that suit the number of guests and available preparation time.

Menu planning framework

A balanced festive menu can include:

  • one rice or grain dish;
  • one bread such as puri, roti or regional flatbread;
  • one or two vegetable dishes;
  • dal, sambar, kadhi or another protein-rich accompaniment;
  • a cooling item such as raita where appropriate;
  • one snack or farsan;
  • one or two sweets;
  • fruit or prasad;
  • tea, coffee, chaas or another beverage.

Sweets and regional favorites

Depending on region, families may serve jalebi, kheer, payasam, shrikhand, puran poli, laddoo, halwa, sundal, festive rice dishes or other local sweets and snacks. In Gujarat, fafda-jalebi is popularly associated with Dussehra. In Maharashtra, puran poli and festive meals may be part of family celebrations. Southern households may prepare sundal, payasam and other Navratri/Vijayadashami foods.

Fasting and dietary needs

Fasting practices vary widely. Do not assume everyone follows the same restrictions. If hosting guests, label dishes containing nuts, dairy, gluten or other common allergens and keep vegetarian or fasting-compatible dishes separate where needed.

Make-ahead strategy

Prepare sweets, dry snacks, chutneys and some components one day ahead. On the festival day, focus on fresh breads, final tempering, garnishing and puja-related offerings. This keeps the meal festive without making the host spend the entire day in the kitchen.

Cooking and serving tips

Scale recipes based on the number of guests before shopping. For sweets, make smaller test batches if you are trying a recipe for the first time. Keep dairy desserts refrigerated, use clean serving utensils, and separate foods for guests with allergies or fasting restrictions.

For a large gathering, choose one showpiece sweet and keep the rest of the menu simple.

Common mistakes to avoid

A frequent mistake is presenting one regional custom as if it represents all of India. Another is copying ritual instructions without knowing whether they belong to the family's own tradition.

Accuracy improves when you distinguish scripture, later retellings, folklore, local custom and modern festival practice. For public events, never trade safety for a better view or photograph.

Frequently asked questions

What should I know about Sundal Recipes for Navratri and Vijayadashami?

Sundal is a quick stir-fry of boiled legumes (chickpeas, black chana, green gram or peanuts) with a mustard and curry-leaf tempering and fresh coconut. In Tamil Nadu it is offered each evening to Golu visitors through Navaratri and on Vijayadashami. A basic chickpea sundal recipe is below.

Is Dussehra celebrated the same way everywhere?

No. Dussehra and Vijayadashami traditions vary substantially by region, language, family and religious tradition.

Is Dussehra the same as Vijayadashami?

The terms often refer to the same festival day, but regional usage and the traditions emphasized can differ.

Does every Dussehra celebration include Ravana Dahan?

No. Ravana Dahan is prominent in many north Indian celebrations, but many communities observe Vijayadashami without burning an effigy.

How can families keep the celebration simple?

Choose the customs that are meaningful to your household: a short puja, blessings from elders, a festive meal, reading or storytelling, or attending one community event.

What is the best way to learn the local tradition?

Ask family elders or community organizers and consult reputable temple, cultural or regional sources rather than assuming a generic checklist applies everywhere.

Final thoughts

Like every part of Dussehra and Vijayadashami, this is best understood in the festival’s wider context rather than as an isolated custom. The festival's strength lies in its diversity: families and communities can honor inherited traditions while still adapting celebration details to modern life, safety needs and local circumstances.
