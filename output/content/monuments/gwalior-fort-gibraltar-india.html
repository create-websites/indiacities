A Confluence of Styles

The temples in Gwalior Fort are stunning examples of architecture, blending North and South Indian styles seamlessly.

Teli Ka Mandir (c. 9th Century)

The Oilman's Temple

This ancient temple, the fort's oldest monument, is renowned for its distinctive design. It blends a Nagara-style base from North India with a Dravidian barrel-vaulted roof from the South, showcasing a rare fusion of traditions. Initially devoted to Vishnu, it was later repurposed as a Shaivite shrine.

Saas Bahu Temples (c. 11th Century)

The Mother-in-law & Daughter-in-law Temples

Two finely crafted temples dedicated to Vishnu stand side by side the grand 'Saas' (Mother-in-law) temple and the petite 'Bahu' (Daughter-in-law) temple. Both showcase Kachchhapaghata artistry, adorned with intricate carvings of deities and mythological tales.

The Builders of the Fort

The fort's identity was shaped by two key eras: the Hindu Tomar Rajputs, known for crafting elegant palaces, and the Mughals, who transformed it into a powerful state prison.
