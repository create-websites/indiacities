History & Overview

Agartala, the capital of Tripura, is a city in North Eastern India known for its diverse cultural heritage. Originally a part of a Hindu kingdom, it was conquered by the Mughals in 1733 and later became a British territory in 1808. After India gained independence, Tripura was established as a state in 1972, with Agartala as its capital.

Situated on the Haora River, the city is a mere 2 km away from the Bangladesh border and is famous for its contemporary twin palaces, Ujjayanta and Kunjaban.

Top Sightseeing Spots

Ujjayanta Palace

An impressive Indo-Saracenic estate constructed in 1901 by Maharaja Radhakishore Manikya, nestled within 28 hectares of lush park grounds

Kunjaban Palace

Constructed in 1917, this palace atop a hill acts as the designated home for the Governor of Tripura.

Neermahal

Situated 55 kilometers from its nearest town, the one-of-a-kind 'Water Palace' is nestled in the heart of Rudrasagar Lake, showcasing a fusion of Hindu and Mugh

Unakoti

An ancient pilgrimage destination showcasing extensive rock-carved sculptures from the 8th century, along with a towering 30-foot statue honoring Lord Shiva.
