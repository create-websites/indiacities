Jammu: City of Temples

A serene and sacred city nestled in the foothills of the Himalayas.

The Legend of Raja Jambu Lochan

Jammu was named after its discoverer, King Raja Jambu Lochan, following a remarkable hunting trip.

presence of his court. Tawi RiverThe serene scene inspired him to create a city where all living beings coexisted without enmity, resulting in the creation of countless temples that have made Jammu known as the 'City of Temples.'

Renowned Shrines

Raghunath Mandir

Situated in the center of the city, this complex, constructed in 1857, is regarded as the region's crowning glory. It stands as the biggest temple complex in North India.

  • Interiors: Plated with gold.
  • Library: Features rare Sanskrit manuscripts and texts.
  • Deities: Dedicated to Lord Vishnu and Lord Rama.
  • Architecture: Shows strong Mughal influence.

Bawely Mata Mandir (Mahakali Mandir)

Situated inside the Bahu FortThis temple, built in 1822 after Maharaja Gulab Singh's coronation, is devoted to Mahamaya, the Dogras' local deity, who is revered as second only to Mata Vaishno Devi.

Special pilgrim attraction on Tuesdays and Sundays.

Shrines of Lord Shiva

Ranbireshwar Temple

The collection includes 12 crystal Shivalingams, varying in size from 12 to 18 feet, along with thousands of saligrams displayed in the galleries.

Panchbakhtar & Peer Kho

Ancient temples dedicated to Lord Shiva situated alongside the river.

World-Renowned Pilgrimages

Vaishno Devi

Attracts thousands of pilgrims every year. A visit to Bhairav Ghati is required to complete the pilgrimage.
