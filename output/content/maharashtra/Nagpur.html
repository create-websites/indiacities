Nagpur: The Orange City

The verdant heart of India and the distinguished former capital of Maharashtra.

City Overview

Established in 1702 by King Bakht Buland Shah of Deogad, Nagpur functions as the second capital of MaharashtraNamed for the River Nag, it is known as the second most eco-friendly city in India and is renowned worldwide for its exceptional oranges.

Area: 9892 sq. km
Best Time: Sept to April
Marathi, Hindi, English
STD Code: 0712

Gems of Nagpur

Dheekshabhoomi

A revered Buddhist pilgrimage destination where Dr. B.R. Ambedkar converted to Buddhism in 1956, attracting thousands of visitors every year for the Ashok Vijaya Dashmi celebration.

Ramtek

Historical location where Kalidasa wrote 'Meghdoot', housing ancient Ram and Jain temples with stunning carvings and sculptures dating back 600 years.

Sevagram

Founded in 1933 by Mahatma Gandhi, this ashram was the central hub for his social service initiatives for 15 years.

Markanda

Located along the Vainganga River, this group of 24 temples showcases architectural designs closely resembling those of the Khajuraho temples.

Orange Specialty & Wildlife

The Orange Heritage

In 1896, Raghujiraje Bhonsle initiated orange cultivation in this region. Currently, Vidarbha cultivates oranges on 80,000 hectares, yielding almost 5 lakh tons each year, making it a profitable crop for India's foreign exchange earnings.

Pench National Park

This park, also known as Jawaharlal Nehru Udyan, is situated 86 km away from Nagpur. It was established around the Pench Irrigation project and is a famous spot for day trips and wildlife watching.
