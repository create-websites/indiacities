Kurukshetra: Land of Gita

The historic battleground of the Mahabharata, a pivotal aspect of Indian spiritual tradition.

Mythological & Historical Significance

Kurukshetra is famously known as Dharmakshetra, where the Kauravas and Pandavas fought the 18-day Mahabharata battle. It is a holy site where Lord Krishna imparted his teachings. Karma theory to Arjuna, known today as the holy Bhagavad Gita.

Named after King Kuru
Home to the Holy Gita Theory
Part of Emperor Harsha's Golden Age
Sacred to all ten Sikh Gurus

Major Attractions

Religious Landmarks

  • Jyotisar: The exact spot where the Gita was delivered.
  • Brahmsarovar: A vast, holy water tank for pilgrims.
  • Sannihit Tank: Believed to be the meeting point of seven holy Saraswatis.
  • Bhishm Kund & Baan-Ganga: Sites tied directly to epic battle events.

Culture & Heritage

  • Sri Krishna Museum: Artifacts depicting the life of Lord Krishna.
  • Gurudwara 6th Pathshahi: Commemorating visits from Sikh Gurus.
  • Tomb of Sheikh Chaheli: A stunning Sufi saint memorial.
  • Sthaneshvar Mahadev: An ancient temple of great religious power.

Archaeological Interest

The area is rich in ancient relics, such as Painted Greyware pottery discovered at locations such as Asthipura, Raja Karan ka Tila, Bhagpura, and Bhor Saidan.

Modern Kurukshetra

The city boasts Kurukshetra University, a top-notch Science Museum, and bustling commercial mandies, seamlessly merging ancient tradition with contemporary advancement.

Geography & Climate

Covering an area of 1,682 square kilometers, the district is situated in the plains of the Saraswati, Ghaggar, and Markanda rivers. This location provides rich alluvial soil to the region.

Summer: Hot (~45°C)
Winter: Cold (~3°C)

Travel Guide

By Rail

Well-connected with major Indian cities through a robust railway network.

By Road

The district is well-connected by road to the rest of Haryana and nearby states thanks to its excellent transportation links.

Administration

Separated from Karnal in 1973; includes sub-states Pehowa and Thanesar.
