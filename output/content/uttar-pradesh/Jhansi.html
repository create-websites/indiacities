Jhansi Tourism

The Legendary Land of the Gallant Queen, Maharani Laxmi Bai.

Overview of Jhansi

Jhansi, a well-known district in Northern India's Uttar Pradesh, covers 5,024 sq KM and includes five tehsils and eight blocks. It is renowned for its rich heritage and is particularly famous for Maharani Laxmi Bai's courageous leadership against the British.

Jhansi has developed into a key infrastructure hub in recent times, serving as the crossroads for the North-South and East-West corridors. This prime location has driven a surge in real estate development and the proposal for a new Greenfield airport.

Historical Legacy

Originally named Balwant Nagar during the rule of the Chandela kings, the region rose to prominence again in 1613 with the construction of the Fort of Jhansi by Raja Bir Singh Deo of Orchha.

After defeating Mohammad Khan Bangash in 1729, Maratha Peshva Bajirao I was rewarded with a part of the Bundela state, transforming Jhansi into a Maratha Suba. This city would later play a pivotal role in the 1857 revolt, as Rani Laxmi Bai led a famous resistance against British colonial rule due to the doctrine of lapse.

Major Attractions

The Fort of Jhansi

Built on a rugged hill, this enormous fortress boasts ten gates and a wall that once encircled the whole city.

Government Museum

Contains a wide array of sculptures, paintings, and memorials from the 1857 mutiny, all crafted from metal scrap.

Shrine of St. Jude

A significant Catholic site where St. Jude's remains are interred, hosting a yearly week-long celebration in October.

Modern Infrastructure

Explore India's main corridors to witness the swift urban growth of the district.
