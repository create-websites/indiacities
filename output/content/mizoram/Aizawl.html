Aizawl: Home of the Highlanders

A beautiful capital city graced with exotic charm and a vibrant tribal history.

Quick Facts

Area: 110 sq.km
Altitude: 3500 feet above sea level
Best Time: October to March
Languages: Mizo, English
Summer: 20°C - 30°C
Winter: 11°C - 21°C

An Overview of Aizawl

Aizawl, the largest city and capital of Mizoram, is home to over a quarter of the state's population. The name 'Aizawl' means "the home of the Highlanders."

The majority of Mizos are followers of Christianity, and Mizoram has an 87% literacy rate, which is the second highest in India. Mizoram was historically a part of Assam until 1972 when it became a Union Territory, and it achieved full statehood in 1987.

Places to Visit

The State Museum

A remarkable collection showcasing the rich history and culture of the Mizo people, including traditional attire, tools, and historical artifacts.

Mini Zoo

Located in a scenic setting, this facility is home to various rare animal species, with a special focus on the endangered Sun Bear.

Tamdil Lake

This natural lake, situated 85 kilometers away from Aizawl, is a well-liked destination for picnics and is highly recommended for its tranquil beauty.

Vantawng Falls

Located 137 kilometers from the city, these falls in Mizoram are the biggest and most important, set amidst dense bamboo forests.

Murlen National Park

Located near the Indo-Myanmar border, 245 kms east of Aizawl, this park is a biodiverse haven, home to Tigers, Himalayan Bears, Sambar Deer, Barking Deer, Serow, as well as a wide range of orchids and birds.

Fairs and Festivals

Chapchar Kut

Feb-March: Celebrates the clearing of jungles for sowing.

Mim Kut

September: Maize harvest festival in honor of departed souls.

Pawl Kut

December: A grand harvest festival celebrated with traditional pomp.

Travel Guide

By Air

Lengpui Airport (45 km away). Regular flights connect to Kolkata.

By Rail

Nearest Railhead is Silchar (Assam), located 180 km from Aizawl.

By Road

Buses ply from Silchar via National Highway No. 44.
