Jalandhar

The ancient city of Punjab, fusing age-old history with contemporary industrial prowess.

City Overview

Located in the center of Punjab, Jalandhar District is unquestionably ancient and is considered to be the oldest city in the state.

Currently, Jalandhar is a thriving industrial hub and the leading manufacturer of top-quality sports equipment in India. The city boasts a harmonious mix of historical sites, sacred places, and contemporary entertainment venues.

Key Attractions

Devi Talab Mandir

An updated temple complex showcases a golden Mandir surrounded by a lake and is famous worldwide for hosting the annual 'Hariballabh Sangeet Sammelan' music festival every December.

Pushpa Gujral Science City

The biggest and second Science City in India, this destination offers a blend of entertainment and education with interactive science displays for visitors of all ages.

Wonderland Theme Park

An 11-acre amusement park with water attractions, featuring exciting rides and games suitable for families of all ages.

St Mary's Cathedral Church

Situated in Jalandhar Cantonment, this cathedral showcases a unique 'cosmo-culture' design, making it the sole one of its kind in the East.

Religious & Historical Legacy

Imam Nasir Mausoleum

A stunningly crafted mausoleum, dating back 800 years, is situated in the city center, next to a 400-year-old Jamma Masjid.

Shiv Mandhir & Tulsi Mandir

Shiv Mandhir, built during the Lodhi Era, showcases a distinctive mix of Hindu and Muslim architectural styles. Tulsi Mandir, previously known as the Temple of Vrinda, is a historic landmark in the city.

Desh-Bhagat Memorial Hall

A memorial hall built to commemorate the martyrs who gave their lives in the Indian fight for independence from British rule.

Fairs & Festivals

Hariballabh Sangeet Sammelan

An esteemed annual musical event has been held at Devi Talab Mandir for more than 125 years.

Sodal Mandir Fair

Thousands of devotees come together during the month of September for 'Anand Chaturdashi' to seek blessings from the child deity.
