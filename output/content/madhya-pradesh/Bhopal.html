Bhopal: City of Lakes

A mix of picturesque natural landscapes, ancient Islamic structures, and contemporary cultural sites.

Overview

capital city of Madhya Pradesh, Bhopal is renowned for being City of Lakes Known for its stunning natural beauty and impressive mosques, the city center boasts a pair of picturesque lakes and is home to India's largest mosque, the Taj-ul-Masjid.

Altitude: 523 meters
Annual Rainfall: 1083 mm
Best Time: October to March
Hindi, Urdu, English

History

Bhopal's roots can be traced back to the 11th century AD during the reign of King Bhoja. The city as we know it today was established in 1723 by Dost Mohammad, an Afghan soldier, after the fall of the Mughal Empire.

Bhopal, the second-largest Muslim state under British rule, remained independent until 1949. It became part of Madhya Pradesh in 1956 and was named its capital.

Note: The city is infamous for the 1984 Gas Tragedy, where a leak of methyl isocyanate at the Union Carbide plant led to numerous fatalities and long-term health issues for many.

Major Attractions

Taj-ul-Masjid

Known as 'The Crown of Mosques', this pink portico structure with two white domes is the largest mosque in India, hosting the renowned Ijtima festival every year.

Upper & Lower Lakes

Split by a bridge, these areas serve as central locations for activities such as sailing, motorboating, and paddle boating. The Upper Lake is well-known for its evening shikara rides.

Laxmi Narayan Temple

Also called Birla Mandir, this temple can be found on Arera Hills. Nearby, the museum contains a diverse assortment of sculptures from different districts in Madhya Pradesh.

Moti Masjid

Constructed in 1860 by Sikandar Begum, this mosque, made of red sandstone, is a significant example of Islamic architecture featuring a marble front and tall minarets.

Heritage & Museums

Shaukat Mahal & Sardar Manzil

Sardar Manzil is an extraordinary blend of Renaissance and Gothic architectural styles, originally used as a public audience hall by the ruling class.

Rashtriya Manav Sangrahalaya

The 195-acre National Museum of Man is renowned for its exhibition of India's tribal folk arts and anthropological heritage.

How to Reach

By Air

Bhopal Airport is situated around 12 kilometers away from the Old City.

By Rail

Shatabdi Express connects this key station on the Delhi-Madras route to Delhi, as well as Mumbai, Gwalior, and Ujjain.
