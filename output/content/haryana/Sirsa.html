A Tour to Sirsa

Exploring the ancient origins of 'Sairishaka' in the western region of Haryana.

Historical Legacy

Sirsa is the farthest west town in Haryana, formerly known in ancient times as Sairishaka The town, originally founded in the 7th century AD by King 'Saras' who constructed its original fort, has also been historically referred to in the Mahabharata. Sarsuti and Sirsapattan.

Known for Cotton & Citrus production
323 Villages across 4 Tehsils
Birthplace of Lt. Devi Lal Chaudhary
Ancient fort ruins from 7th Century AD

Places of Interest

Archaeological Mounds

  • Sikanderpur: Home to stone sculptures of Lord Shiva and Indra, as well as Rang Mahal pottery.
  • Arnian Wali: A 10-foot high mound containing ancient pottery remains.
  • Suchan & Mangiana: Sites featuring medieval wares and ancient archaeological specimens.

Spiritual Landmarks

  • Dera Sacha Sauda: Constructed in 1948 by Saint Shah Mastana, this building includes a spacious hall and 600 rooms.
  • Dera Baba Sarsai Nath: A temple of historical significance where Emperor Shah Jahan prayed for his son's well-being.
  • Saint Baba Bihari Samadhi: A peaceful samadhi located within a beautiful western garden.

Historical Graves & Temples

Tomb of Khawaja Pir

This site holds great significance as it was visited by Khawaja Abdul Shankar alongside Mohammed Ghori, and is also believed to have been the residence of Guru Nanak Dev for 40 days.

Dera Baba Sarsai Nath Temple

Emperor Shah Jahan gifted the land and dome for the construction of temples dedicated to Shiva and Durga as an offering for his son's blessings.

Travel Info

By Road

Sirsa is well-connected to major cities in the region through a wide network of bus facilities.

By Rail

Ramgondam is the closest major station, situated 66 km away from the city.

By Air

Hyderabad, located 268 km away, boasts a well-connected major airport that serves India's major cities.
