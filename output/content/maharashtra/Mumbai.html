Mumbai City Trip

The 'Film City of India' where contemporary culture blends with the Arabian Sea coastline.

City Overview

Mumbai, formerly known as BombayNamed after 'Mumbadevi,' the guardian deity of the Koli fishermen, Mumbai, the capital of Maharashtra, is a thriving metropolis with a diverse population, including Gujaratis, South Indians, Parsis, and Sindhis, making up around half of its inhabitants.

State: Maharashtra
Climate: Humid (Summer Max 34°C)
Best Time: October to February
Marathi, Hindi, English, Gujarati

History

Upon India's independence in 1947, Bombay became the capital of Bombay StateIn 1960, the state was dissolved, resulting in the formation of Maharashtra with Bombay as its capital. The city then officially changed its name to Mumbai in 1995.

Major Landmarks

Gateway of India

Constructed in tribute to the arrival of King George V and Queen Mary, the monument stands as a majestic icon of the city and a popular landmark for tourists.

Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus

Previously known as Victoria Terminus, this iconic railway station now serves as the headquarters of Central Railway and remains one of the most bustling stations in India.

Haji Ali Mosque

This mosque, honoring saint Haji Ali, is situated in the middle of the sea and can only be reached via a narrow path during low tide.

Flora Fountain

An intricately decorated stone fountain in the Fort area showcases a statue of the Roman Goddess of Flowers, Flora.

Culture & Cuisine

Mumbai is known for its diverse mix of ethnicities, renowned for its distinctive characteristics. "Bambaiyaa Hindi" dialect and Indo-western culture.

Street Food

The city's soul lies in its roadside fast food, specifically Vada Pav and Bhelpuri, alongside Chinese and South Indian staples.

Beverages

Tea ranks as the top choice at stalls on every street corner, closely followed by coffee, coconut water, and fruit juices.

Travel Guide

By Air

The flight network in Mumbai connects to many major international cities, sometimes even surpassing the traffic in New Delhi.

By Rail

Connected through Central (East/North) and Western (North India) Railways via key stations such as CST and Churchgate.

By Road

Conveniently connected to all parts of India through National Highways 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, and 17.

Local Spots

  • Powai Lake (Picnic spot)
  • Aarey Milk Colony
  • Nehru Centre & Planetarium
  • Vasai Fort (Strategic Ruins)
