Nasik: The Grape City

The Wine Capital of India and a sacred pilgrimage destination along the Godavari River.

City Overview

city of grapes, Nasik is situated on the western edge of the Deccan Peninsula within the Western Ghats. 'Wine Capital of India'It is renowned as one of India's four most sacred sites and is well-known for its hosting of the Kumbh Mela It occurs every 12 years and has a strong connection to the Ramayana, believed to be the location where Lord Rama resided during his period of exile.

185 km from Mumbai | 202 km from Pune
Altitude: 225 meters
Best Time: October to February
Marathi, Hindi, English

History

Nasik was once the capital of the powerful Satavahana dynasty It was a crucial trading hub and known as 'Gulshanabad' during the Mughal era, reverting back to its original name in 1751 during the Peshwa rule.

The city was instrumental in India's fight for independence, being the site of important movements led by Mahatma Gandhi and Dr. Ambedkar's temple entry movement in 1932.

Major Attractions

Kumbh Mela

Every 12 years, millions of devotees gather at the world's largest religious event to take a ritual holy dip in the Godavari River for spiritual purification.

Panchawati

Situated in the northern region of Nasik, this site is known as the place of five Banyan trees. It holds significance as the abode of Lord Rama and Sita during their period of exile.

Sundarnarayan Temple

Constructed in 1756 by Gangadhar Yashwant Chandrachud, this temple is devoted to Lord Vishnu and is located in close proximity to the Ahillyabai Holkar Bridge.

Sita Gumpha

A narrow staircase leads to an ancient cave in Panchawati, where idols of Rama, Laxman, and Sita stand, marking the spot of Sita's reported abduction.

Sacred Spots

  • Ramkund

    A sacred pool within the Godavari river where legend has it that Rama once bathed, often referred to as the 'Ganges of the South'.

  • Kalaram Temple

    Constructed in 1794 using distinctive black stones, this temple holds great importance in the Panchawati region.

  • Modakeshwar Ganesh Temple

    Well-known for its 'Swayambhu' (self-manifested) Lord Ganesh idol, named after his beloved sweet, the Modak.

Travel Guide

By Air

The closest airport is Mumbai International Airport, which is situated 185 kilometers from the location.

By Rail

A major Central Railway station. The Panchvati Express connects to Mumbai in just 4 hours.

By Road

Easily accessible by private and state buses from Mumbai, Thane, and Pune.
