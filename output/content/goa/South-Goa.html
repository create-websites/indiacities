South Goa

A serene sanctuary of pristine beaches and rich colonial heritage.

District Overview

The southern region of Goa spans across 1,966 square kilometers and is famous for its picturesque and unspoiled beaches. The captivating beauty of its shoreline is a primary attraction for tourists in search of serenity.

South Goa is divided into three natural regions: the Low lands (coastal line), Plateaus, and Mountain region, rich in natural resources such as iron and manganese ore, exported to countries like Japan, South Korea, and various European nations.

Margao: The Cultural Heart

Margao, the commercial hub of Salcete taluka in South Goa, is the district headquarters and second-largest city in Goa. It still bears traces of its Portuguese colonial history and is thought to have derived its name from 'Mathgram,' alluding to the Hindu religious centers that were prominent in the area in the past.

The town is a prime illustration of the seamless blend of Portuguese and Hindu influences, with enduring Hindu traditions and a thriving cultural and economic center nestled in lush farmland despite years of colonization.

Natural Divisions & Tourism

Coastal Low Lands

The coastline spans 110 kilometers and showcases a beautiful array of beaches such as Bogmalo, Velsao, Majorda, Colva, Benaulim, Betul, Agonda, and the renowned Palolem Beach.

Plateau Lands

Situated amidst the mountains and the coast, these terrains vary in elevation from 30 to 100 meters. Rich in laterite stone, a crucial material for building traditional houses in the area.

The Mountain Region

The eastern part of South Goa is dominated by the Sahyadri MountainsThis area is characterized by dense forests, steep peaks, and significant natural landmarks.

Tradition and Festivals

South Goa's festivals are lively celebrations that bring together all residents. Ganesh Chaturthi recognized as the top festival in the area, characterized by the homecoming of many Goans to their ancestral homes to gather with extended family.

Goan traditions have maintained their distinct character while the Mandovi and Zuari rivers continue to play a crucial role as waterways, historically ferrying people and now transporting ore to Mormugao harbor.

Must-See Places

Top Beaches

Colva Beach and Palolem Beach offer the epitome of South Goa's coastal charm.

Historical Interest

Discover the Arvalem caves and the historical colonial buildings in Margao.

Waterways

Observe the ore being transported through the Mandovi and Zuari rivers towards Mormugao harbor.
