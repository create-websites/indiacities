Calicut: The City of Spices

A historic coastal treasure where world explorers first arrived on Indian land.

Overview

Located on the Malabar Coast Calicut (Kozhikode) in Northern Kerala is a stunning city known for its beaches, backwaters, and rich history.

The city's backwaters are a self-sustaining ecosystem of lakes, canals, and estuaries, created by 44 rivers flowing into the Arabian Sea. This network, exclusive to Kerala, plays a crucial role in connecting villages through local transport.

Historical Landmarks

Kappakkadavu Beach

Also known as Kappad BeachOn May 27, 1498, Vasco da Gama made his historic arrival in India, marked by a small stone monument.

The Thali Temple

An age-old Shiva temple built during the Zamorin reign, which was later damaged by Tipu Sultan and Hyder Ali, underwent restoration in the 18th century and now contains sanctuaries for Ganapati and Narasimha.

Pazhassiraja Museum

Managed by the State Archaeological Department, it contains ancient coins, mural paintings, and bronzes, while a neighboring art gallery showcases the masterpieces of Raja Ravi Varma.

Mananchira

This area was once the palace tank of King Mana Vikrama, but now it has been converted into a beautiful display of traditional architecture, complete with lush green lawns and a captivating musical fountain.

Nature & Science

Kadalundi Bird Sanctuary

Spread across 3 km, this sanctuary is a refuge for 100 resident species and 60 migratory species, such as terns, gulls, and herons, who stop by from November to April.

Dolphin's Point

An idyllic location where visitors can catch a glimpse of dolphins frolicking in the sea during the early morning hours. The beach is also home to a lighthouse and the Calicut Town Center.

Science Planetarium

Situated in Jaffarkhan Colony, this facility offers a range of scientific instruments, puzzles, and a planetarium to explore the wonders of the cosmos.
