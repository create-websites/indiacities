Chamba Tour

The historic capital of the Pahari Kings, situated on the shores of the River Ravi.

Historical Overview

Situated in Himachal Pradesh on the banks of the river RaviChamba, formerly the capital of the Pahari Kings, was named after Princess Champavati by her father, King Sahil Verma.

Chamba is known for its beautiful handicrafts and historic temples, where social customs and traditional ways of life have been carefully maintained. The city is also inhabited by ancient tribes like the Gaddies and Pangwalas.

Ancient Shrines

The intricately carved temples of Chamba are reminiscent of those in Rajasthan. A distinctive aspect of Chamba's spiritual setting is a line of three Vishnu and three Shiva temples near the palace, each showcasing unique shikaras or spires.

The Laxmi Narayan Temple holds the title of being the city's oldest and most intricately carved shrine.

Major Attractions

Chaughan

A spacious grassy area in the heart of the city serves as a hub for trading and public events, including the annual Minjar Procession, a significant cultural and athletic celebration.

Bhuri Singh Museum

Preserving Chamba's rich cultural legacy, the residence showcases miniature paintings from the Kangra and Basholi schools, as well as murals rescued from the Rang Mahal Palace.

Akhand Chandi Palace

Easily identified by its unique green roof, this once royal home is now used as a Government College and District Library.

Bharmaur

Chamba's ancient capital is enveloped by dense forests and is the site of the Lakshmi Devi, Narsingh, and Ganesh temples.

Natural Regions

Pangi (Chenab Valley)

A barren and icy land where the Chandrabhaga river winds through jagged mountains, enclosed by the towering Great Himalayas and Pir Panjal ranges.

Wilderness Areas

Discover the picnic spots and sheep breeding farms in Sarol, or venture to Killar to explore the deep gorges of the Chenab River and visit the historical rest houses in Purthi.
