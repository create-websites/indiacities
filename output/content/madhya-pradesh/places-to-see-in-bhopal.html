Ujjain: The Greenwich of India

An ancient city nestled along the holy Shipra River, where history and spirituality intertwine.

Overview

Situated on the western side of Madhya Pradesh, Ujjain is a revered holy city in India with a rich history. Formerly known as Ujjayini, it is often called the 'Greenwich of India' and was once a prominent hub for mathematical and astronomical studies, boasting legendary scholars such as Brahmagupta and Varahamihira.

Altitude: 212 meters above sea level
Annual Rainfall: 152 cms
Best Time: October to March
Languages: Hindi, English

History & Heritage

Ujjain has a long history, as it was once the home of Ashoka during his time as viceroy of the western provinces of the Maurya Empire. It was also the capital of King Vikramaditya, whose court was known for fostering great literary talents like the poet Kalidas among the 'nine jewels' of Sanskrit literature.

The city served as the residence of the Maratha leader Sindhia in the late 18th century before being incorporated into Gwalior state. After India gained independence, Ujjain was eventually included in Madhya Pradesh in 1956.

Sacred Attractions

Maha Kumbh Mela

Every twelve years, pilgrims and sadhus flock to the sacred Shipra River for the grand bathing rituals in large numbers.

Mahakaleshwar Temple

Devoted to Lord Shiva, this temple is home to one of the 12 holy Jyotirlingas. It consists of five levels, with one section underground illuminated by brass lamps.

Bade Ganeshji Ka Mandir

It is home to one of India's largest statues of Lord Ganesha, as well as a distinctive Panchmukhi image of Lord Hanuman.

Cultural Landmarks

  • Vikram Kirti Mandir: A cultural hub showcasing the Scindia Oriental Research Institute and its collection of more than 18,000 rare manuscripts.
  • Kailadeh Palace: Constructed in 1458 by Mandu Rulers on an island in the Shipra river, utilizing Persian architectural techniques.

Travel Guide

By Air

The nearest airport is at Indore (55 km), with connections to Delhi, Mumbai, and Bhopal.

By Rail

A crucial stop on the Western Railway network, with connections to numerous major cities across India.

By Road

Linked by regular state transport buses to nearly every city in Madhya Pradesh.
