Allahabad Tour

Step into the King of Tirthas, where history and holiness intertwine.

The Holy Sangam

Allahabad, also known as Sangam or Prayag, meaning 'place of sacrifice' and 'confluence', holds a special place as the meeting point of the Ganges, Yamuna, and Saraswati rivers. According to Hindu mythology, this land was chosen by Lord Brahma for the Prakrishta Yajna.

Site of Annual Magha Mela
One of the holiest cities in India

The Legend of Kumbh Mela

Legend has it that Vishnu spilled four drops of amrita (nectar) from a kumbh (pot) during a scuffle among the gods, which landed on earth at the tirthas: Prayag, Haridwar, Nasik, and Ujjain.

The Kumbh Mela, a grand event held every three years, honors the Sangam as Tirtharaja, the 'King of Tirthas'. Its Mela, occurring once every twelve years, is revered as the most sacred and significant of all gatherings.

Allahabad Fort

Formerly one of the top garrisons in the Mughal Empire, this site is home to the Ashokan Pillar (232 BC), Saraswati Kup, and the Jodhabai Palace. Only a small portion is accessible to visitors.

Anand Bhawan

The former residence of Jawaharlal Nehru and Indira Gandhi, now a museum, is a historic landmark.

Hanuman Mandir

The Sangam boasts a one-of-a-kind temple where the idol of Lord Hanuman reclines, the only one of its kind in North India.

Patalpuri Temple

An ancient temple located beneath the fort is home to the Akshaya Vat, a legendary immortal tree, and was once visited by the Chinese traveler Hiuen Tsang.

Historic Parks & Gardens

  • Alfred Park: The largest park in Allahabad, holding significant historical value.

  • Minto Park: Now recognized as Madan Mohan Malaviya Park, a notable feature in the city's history.

  • Khusrau Bagh: Houses the tomb of Prince Khusru, son of Emperor Jahangir.

At a Glance

Shankar Viman Mandapam

A 130-feet high structure featuring four distinct floors.

Ashoka Pillar

At a height of 10.6 meters, the structure showcases Persian inscriptions detailing Emperor Jahangir.

Travel Tip

The Hanuman Mandir is flooded when the Ganges river overflows.
