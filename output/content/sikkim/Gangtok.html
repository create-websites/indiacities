Gangtok: The Hillside Haven

A harmonious blend of ancient Buddhist traditions and 21st-century modernity.

Quick Facts

Area: 954 sq km

Best Time: Feb-May & Sept-Nov
Languages: English, Nepali, Hindi, Tibetan

History of Gangtok

Gangtok, the capital city of Sikkim, is located 110 kilometers from Siliguri and is a melting pot of various cultures and religions coexisting peacefully. The history of Gangtok can be traced back to the establishment of the secluded Gangtok monastery in 1716. Over time, the town grew in significance, becoming a pilgrimage center with the construction of the Enchey Monastery in 1840.

In 1894, Thutob Namgyal moved the capital from Tumlong to Gangtok, elevating its significance. After India gained independence in 1947, Sikkim emerged as a sovereign state with Gangtok as its capital, before eventually merging with India in 1975.

Cultural Tapestry

The city embraces a diverse range of festivals, from Hindu celebrations like Diwali and Makar Sankranti to Buddhist festivals such as Losar (Tibetan New Year), Saga Dawa, and Lhabab Duechen. Music is deeply ingrained in the local culture, with a mix of Western rock, Hindi pop, and indigenous Nepali rock enjoyed by residents.

Noodle enthusiasts enjoy dishes such as Thukpa, Chowmein, and Wontons, while the Momo is a favorite snack. Football and Cricket are the top sports for fans, with the Paljor Stadium serving as the main sporting venue in the city.

Sightseeing In Gangtok

Rumtek Monastery

The eastern Himalayas boast the grandest monastery, constructed by Gyalwa Karmapa in traditional Tibetan style, situated just 24 kilometers from Gangtok.

Institute of Tibetology

A globally recognized hub for studying Mahayana Buddhism, home to a collection of rare Tibetan, Sanskrit, and Lepcha manuscripts as well as more than 200 icons.

Enchey Monastery

Constructed by Lama Druptop Karpo, a tantric master, this two-century-old monastery is renowned for hosting the 'Chaam' masked dance festival every January.

Tsomgo Lake

At an elevation of 3780m, a tranquil lake located 40 km outside the city freezes over in winter and is encircled by colorful alpine flowers in spring.

Dud-Dul Chorten

Constructed in 1945, this Stupa is encircled by 10 prayer wheels (Mani-Lhaskar) and houses sacred texts and mantras.

Deer Park (Rustamji Park)

The park is home to the Red Panda and Himalayan Bear, with a large statue of Lord Buddha and stunning views of the Gangtok hills.

Travel Guide

By Air

The nearest airport is Bagdogra (124 km). Regular buses and flights from Delhi are available.

By Rail

Nearest railheads are New Jalpaiguri (125 km) and Siliguri. Mahananda Exp connects Delhi to the region daily.

By Road

Good transportation links to Darjeeling, Kalimpong, and Siliguri are available through state transport buses that offer frequent services

  • Tasuklakhang-The Royal Chapel
  • Local Momos and Thukpa
