Dussehra and New Beginnings: Business, Education and Personal Goals

Dussehra suits new beginnings because it marks victory and is considered auspicious all day.

By Prashant Dhingra

Read the guide Jump to FAQs

In short

Dussehra suits new beginnings because it marks victory and is considered auspicious all day. Students start courses, businesses open new books or branches, and families buy vehicles or homes. Many people also set one personal goal, such as dropping a habit (their own 'Ravana') or starting a skill, and begin it that day.

Cultural note: Names, stories, rituals and sequencing can differ by region, language, sampradaya, family tradition and local calendar. Where several interpretations exist, this guide presents them as traditions rather than claiming that one version is universally authoritative.

Vijayadashami and auspicious beginnings

Across several Indian traditions, Vijayadashami is considered an auspicious time to begin learning, resume study, honor tools, start a business initiative, make a major purchase or set a new personal goal. The exact ritual logic differs by region and family.

The meaningful idea is gratitude: people honor the books, instruments, machines, vehicles, tools or professional equipment that support knowledge and livelihood.

Ayudha Puja

Ayudha Puja is especially prominent in parts of southern India. "Ayudha" can refer broadly to implements or tools. Traditional observance may include cleaning, decorating and worshipping tools, vehicles, machinery, musical instruments, books or workplace equipment.

In a modern office, this can extend naturally to computers, factory equipment, engineering tools, medical instruments or vehicles. Safety should come first: do not place flowers, powders, liquids or open flames where they could damage electronics or machinery.

Vidyarambham and learning

Vidyarambham marks the ceremonial beginning of learning for young children in parts of southern India, particularly Kerala. A child may be guided to form letters or sacred syllables under the supervision of a teacher, priest, elder or respected guide.

The ritual expresses reverence for knowledge rather than a substitute for regular education.

Shami, Apta and Simollanghan

In parts of western India, families may worship the Shami tree or exchange Apta leaves symbolically as "gold." Simollanghan refers to crossing a boundary and is associated with Dussehra customs in Maharashtra.

These practices are regionally meaningful and should be presented as such rather than as mandatory Dussehra rituals everywhere.

Simple home practice

A practical home observance can include cleaning the items connected to work or study, placing them respectfully near the puja area, offering flowers, taking a moment of gratitude and beginning a constructive new task.

Practical checklist

Before you finalize your plans:

  • confirm the tradition and regional context;
  • verify the festival date or local event schedule;
  • check family, temple, school or venue expectations;
  • prepare clothing, food, puja items or tickets in advance;
  • plan transportation for crowded public events;
  • keep children away from fire, fireworks and dense barriers;
  • choose reusable or low-waste decorations when possible;
  • respect photography restrictions and religious spaces;
  • keep the celebration manageable rather than over-scheduled.

Common mistakes to avoid

A frequent mistake is presenting one regional custom as if it represents all of India. Another is copying ritual instructions without knowing whether they belong to the family's own tradition.

Accuracy improves when you distinguish scripture, later retellings, folklore, local custom and modern festival practice. For public events, never trade safety for a better view or photograph.

Frequently asked questions

What should I know about Dussehra and New Beginnings?

Dussehra suits new beginnings because it marks victory and is considered auspicious all day. Students start courses, businesses open new books or branches, and families buy vehicles or homes. Many people also set one personal goal, such as dropping a habit (their own 'Ravana') or starting a skill, and begin it that day.

Is Dussehra celebrated the same way everywhere?

No. Dussehra and Vijayadashami traditions vary substantially by region, language, family and religious tradition.

Is Dussehra the same as Vijayadashami?

The terms often refer to the same festival day, but regional usage and the traditions emphasized can differ.

Does every Dussehra celebration include Ravana Dahan?

No. Ravana Dahan is prominent in many north Indian celebrations, but many communities observe Vijayadashami without burning an effigy.

How can families keep the celebration simple?

Choose the customs that are meaningful to your household: a short puja, blessings from elders, a festive meal, reading or storytelling, or attending one community event.

What is the best way to learn the local tradition?

Ask family elders or community organizers and consult reputable temple, cultural or regional sources rather than assuming a generic checklist applies everywhere.

Final thoughts

Like every part of Dussehra and Vijayadashami, this is best understood in the festival’s wider context rather than as an isolated custom. The festival's strength lies in its diversity: families and communities can honor inherited traditions while still adapting celebration details to modern life, safety needs and local circumstances.
