Dussehra in Chennai: Golu, Saraswati Puja and Vijayadashami

Chennai celebrates the season with Navaratri Golu, doll displays on stepped platforms that neighbors visit for sundal and gifts.

By Prashant Dhingra

Read the guide Jump to FAQs

In short

Chennai celebrates the season with Navaratri Golu, doll displays on stepped platforms that neighbors visit for sundal and gifts. Saraswati and Ayudha Puja follow on Navami, when books, instruments, tools and vehicles are honored. Vijayadashami is the day for Vidyarambham and new beginnings, and the Golu dolls are put away soon after.

Cultural note: Names, stories, rituals and sequencing can differ by region, language, sampradaya, family tradition and local calendar. Where several interpretations exist, this guide presents them as traditions rather than claiming that one version is universally authoritative.

Why Dussehra looks different across India

Dussehra is one of the clearest examples of how a shared festival date can support many regional traditions. North Indian cities may emphasize Ramlila and Ravana Dahan. Mysuru is known for royal and civic Dasara traditions. Kullu Dussehra centers local deities and begins as the Navratri period concludes. Kerala places strong emphasis on Vidyarambham and learning. Maharashtra includes Simollanghan and exchange of Apta leaves. Tamil Nadu and Karnataka often connect the period with Golu, Saraswati Puja and Ayudha Puja.

The best approach is to describe what is locally important rather than treating one city's program as a model for the whole country.

Common elements to look for

Regional celebrations may include:

  • temple worship and family puja;
  • Ramlila or Ramayana recitation;
  • Ravana effigies and public fairs;
  • processions and decorated tableaux;
  • Durga visarjan and Bijoya gatherings;
  • royal or heritage ceremonies;
  • Ayudha Puja and worship of tools or vehicles;
  • Vidyarambham and education-related rituals;
  • Shami or Apta traditions;
  • folk music, dance, food and seasonal markets.

Respecting local context

Historical practices change over time. Major public festivals may now involve government departments, tourism bodies, security planning, ticketing and large-scale broadcasting. At the same time, neighborhood and family customs may remain intimate and local.

If you are visiting a regional Dussehra celebration, observe local instructions, dress appropriately for religious spaces, ask before photographing rituals or individuals, and do not assume that every public event is primarily a tourist attraction.

Planning a visit

Verify current schedules with official organizers, temple committees, tourism departments or municipal sources. Large Dussehra events can involve road closures, security screening and heavy crowds, so transportation planning matters.

Simple puja planning

A simple home observance may include a clean space, diya or safe electric lamp, flowers, fruit or sweets, water, incense if appropriate, and the books, tools or symbols your family traditionally honors. Exact mantras, offerings and ritual order vary.

If your household follows a specific sampradaya or priestly tradition, use that guidance rather than a generic online sequence. The intention is reverence and gratitude, not collecting the largest possible list of items.

Practical checklist

Before you finalize your plans:

  • confirm the tradition and regional context;
  • verify the festival date or local event schedule;
  • check family, temple, school or venue expectations;
  • prepare clothing, food, puja items or tickets in advance;
  • plan transportation for crowded public events;
  • keep children away from fire, fireworks and dense barriers;
  • choose reusable or low-waste decorations when possible;
  • respect photography restrictions and religious spaces;
  • keep the celebration manageable rather than over-scheduled.

Common mistakes to avoid

A frequent mistake is presenting one regional custom as if it represents all of India. Another is copying ritual instructions without knowing whether they belong to the family's own tradition.

Accuracy improves when you distinguish scripture, later retellings, folklore, local custom and modern festival practice. For public events, never trade safety for a better view or photograph.

Frequently asked questions

What should I know about Dussehra in Chennai?

Chennai celebrates the season with Navaratri Golu, doll displays on stepped platforms that neighbors visit for sundal and gifts. Saraswati and Ayudha Puja follow on Navami, when books, instruments, tools and vehicles are honored. Vijayadashami is the day for Vidyarambham and new beginnings, and the Golu dolls are put away soon after.

Is Dussehra celebrated the same way everywhere?

No. Dussehra and Vijayadashami traditions vary substantially by region, language, family and religious tradition.

Is Dussehra the same as Vijayadashami?

The terms often refer to the same festival day, but regional usage and the traditions emphasized can differ.

Does every Dussehra celebration include Ravana Dahan?

No. Ravana Dahan is prominent in many north Indian celebrations, but many communities observe Vijayadashami without burning an effigy.

How can families keep the celebration simple?

Choose the customs that are meaningful to your household: a short puja, blessings from elders, a festive meal, reading or storytelling, or attending one community event.

What is the best way to learn the local tradition?

Ask family elders or community organizers and consult reputable temple, cultural or regional sources rather than assuming a generic checklist applies everywhere.

Final thoughts

Like every part of Dussehra and Vijayadashami, this is best understood in the festival’s wider context rather than as an isolated custom. The festival's strength lies in its diversity: families and communities can honor inherited traditions while still adapting celebration details to modern life, safety needs and local circumstances.
