The Tale of Two Builders

The fort's legacy was crafted by two renowned Mughal rulers: Akbar forged a sturdy red sandstone stronghold, and Shah Jahan later adorned it as a lavish white marble palace.

A Stage for History

The fort witnessed pivotal moments in Mughal history, from Shivaji's bold encounter to Shah Jahan's sorrowful captivity.

Shivaji's Confrontation (1666)

Chhatrapati Shivaji, the Maratha king, met Aurangzeb at the Diwan-i-Khas but, insulted by his treatment, boldly defied the emperor and orchestrated a daring escape from house arrest in Agra, a turning point in Maratha-Mughal history.

Shah Jahan's Gilded Cage (1658-1666)

Deposed by his son Aurangzeb, Emperor Shah Jahan lived his final eight years imprisoned in the fort's Musamman Burj, an octagonal tower with a marble balcony that overlooked the Taj Mahal, the resting place of his cherished wife.
