Warangal: The Heritage City

Explore the historical capital of the Kakatiya dynasty, a land filled with stone wonders and a wealth of stories.

At a Glance

Area: 12,846 km²
Best Time: November to February
Languages: Telugu, Urdu
Climate: Tropical (Max 39.6°C)
Rainfall: 82.5 cm (Annual)
Old Names: Orugallu, Ekasilanagaram

History & Heritage

Warangal was the ancient capital of the KakatiyasAn Andhra dynasty flourished in the 12th century AD, reaching its peak. The city's name, derived from 'Orugallu' or 'Omtikonda,' is linked to a large boulder-like hillock near the Swayambhu Siva Temple.

Currently, Warangal thrives as a hub for commerce and industry while maintaining a strong connection to its history, known for its stunning lakes, intricate temples, and rich collection of antiques and relics.

Major Attractions

Thousand Pillar Temple

Constructed in 1163 AD by Rudra Deva, this temple showcases exquisite Kakatiya architecture with a star-shaped design. It includes shrines dedicated to Shiva, Vishnu, and Surya, as well as a stunning polished basalt Nandi statue.

Warangal Fort

Dating as far back as the 13th century, this magnificent structure was constructed by Ganapati Deva and Rani Rudramma. Despite being ravaged, the four colossal stone gateways and intricate sculptures continue to display the grandeur of ancient times.

Bhadra Kali Temple

Situated atop a hill, this temple is renowned for its stone statue of Goddess Kali, shown with eight arms holding various weapons.

Ramappa Temple

Also called the Ramalingeswara temple, situated in Palampet, this magnificent monument dating back to 1213 AD is a striking symbol of the opulence of the Kakatiya dynasty.

Excursions & More

Kolanupaka (80 km)

A significant religious center for both Jains and Shaivites, this city served as a secondary capital for the Kalyani Chalukyas and contains a Jain temple featuring a 5-foot Jade statue of Mahavira.

Pakhal Lake (50 km)

A human-made reservoir, finished in 1213 AD, covers an area of 30 square kilometers and was formed by diverting a tributary of the Krishna River.

Sri Veeranarayana Temple

Built in 1104 AD in the Chalukyan style, this temple was initially a Jain shrine before being transformed into a Vaishnava temple.

Travel Guide

By Air

Hyderabad Airport, located 140 kilometers away, has excellent connections to all major Indian metro cities.

By Rail

Warangal is situated on the Chennai-Kazipet-Delhi railway line, connecting it to major cities across India.

By Road

Regular APSRTC bus services from Hyderabad, Vijayawada, Bangalore, and Mysore.

Local Transport

Hanamakonda bus depot offers public transport services and coaches for commuters.
