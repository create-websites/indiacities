The Great Fire of 1828

A mysterious blaze tore through the seven-storey palace, burning for a week. The flames devoured its lavish teak interiors and regal chambers, leaving behind only the stone foundations and fortified walls as echoes of a bygone age.

Comparison of lost elements (timber structures) to surviving ones (stone bases and walls).

A Legacy in Stone and Spirit

Today, Shaniwar Wada stands as a preserved monument and popular tourist spot, its remnants whispering tales of grandeur and sorrow.

The Light and Sound Show

Each evening, a stunning light and sound show within the fort's walls brings the Peshwas' history alive, recounting the Wada's origins, its political significance, and its fall, creating a captivating journey through time for visitors.

The Ghost of Narayanrao

Legend has it that the ghost of young Peshwa Narayanrao, tragically slain within the fort's walls, still lingers. On full moon nights, his desperate cries of 'Kaka, mala wachawa!' ('Uncle, save me!') echo through the halls, a haunting reminder of its grim history.
