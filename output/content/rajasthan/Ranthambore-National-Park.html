Ranthambore National Park

A stunning mix of regal heritage and wild nature in the center of Rajasthan.

Historical Legacy

Ranthambore's rich history is characterized by epic battles and royal hunting traditions. Taken over by Akbar in 1569 AD, it was designated as a wildlife sanctuary in 1955 and officially declared a. Tiger Reserve In 1973, the reserve has grown to include the Sawai Man Singh and Keladevi sanctuaries.

Area: 1,334 sq. km
Best Season: October to March
Rajasthani, Hindi, English
STD Code: 07462

Wildlife & Landmarks

Ranthambore Fort

A marvel from the 10th century, the Chauhan fort in Rajasthan is one of the oldest in the region, boasting towering walls, grand gates, and historic temples built to ward off invaders.

Jogi Mahal

A rest house in the forest with a view of Padam Talab, where an ancient banyan tree, the second largest in India, resides.

The Lakes & Valleys

Padam Talab

The biggest lake in the park, named after the seasonal water lilies, is a prime location for observing wildlife in the early morning.

Raj Bagh Talab

The beautiful lake is speckled with historic ruins and frequented by Sambhar deer and tigers.

Kachida Valley

An area on the edge of the park, characterized by its rough terrain and abundant population of Panthers and Sloth Bears.

Malik Talab

The tiniest lake teeming with bird activity and housing the Indian Marsh Crocodile.

Biodiversity

The park is home to 300 species of birds and 30 species of mammals, thriving in its dry deciduous vegetation.

  • Tigers, Leopards & Hyenas
  • Eagles, Falcons & Kingfishers
  • Pythons, Cobras & Crocodiles
  • Predominant Dhok Plantation
