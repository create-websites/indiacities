Dudhwa National Park

A diverse mix of grasslands, dense Sal forests, and swampy marshes create a vibrant mosaic of habitats[cite:

Park Overview

Area: 490 Sq. km[cite: 467].

Altitude: 150-183 meters[cite: 467].

Rainfall: 1,600 mm[cite: 467].

Best Time: November to April[cite: 467].

Location: India-Nepal border, Uttar Pradesh[cite: 467].

Summers in the area can get scorching, reaching highs of 40 °C, while winters typically range from 20 to 30 °C. The park's southern border is defined by the Suheli River.

History & Conservation

Dudhwa has been designated as a Tiger Reserve since 1879, declared a National Park in 1977, and joined Project Tiger in 1988. Despite the dense forest cover making Tiger sightings rare, the park also safeguards Leopards, Hispid Hares, Swamp Deer (Barasingha), and Rhinos.

The park's success can be attributed to 'Billy' Arjan Singh, whose tireless work was crucial in establishing a sanctuary for Swamp Deer and reintroducing Tigers and Leopards into their natural habitat[cite: 476, 477].

Flora & Fauna

The park covers an area of 811 square kilometers and is divided into two main sections: Dudhwa National Park and Kishanpur Wildlife Sanctuary [cite: 472, 475].

Flora

  • ● Sal (Shorea robusta)
  • ● Shisham (Dalbergia sissoo)
  • ● Khair (Acacia catechu)
  • ● Jamun (Syzygium cumini)

Fauna

  • ● Mammals: Tigers, Leopards, Rhinoceros, Barasingha[cite: 486].
  • ● Birds: 400+ species including Bengal Florican[cite: 486].
  • ● Fishes: 90 identified species[cite: 486].
