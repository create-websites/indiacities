Dussehra Speech for School Assembly

A two-minute Dussehra speech for a school assembly with a greeting, the stories of Rama and Durga, how India celebrates and a closing message about overcoming our own 'Ravanas'.

By Prashant Dhingra

Read the guide Jump to FAQs

In short

A two-minute Dussehra speech for a school assembly with a greeting, the stories of Rama and Durga, how India celebrates and a closing message about overcoming our own 'Ravanas'.

Cultural note: Names, stories, rituals and sequencing can differ by region, language, sampradaya, family tradition and local calendar. Where several interpretations exist, this guide presents them as traditions rather than claiming that one version is universally authoritative.

Dussehra speech (about two minutes)

Good morning to our respected Principal, teachers and my dear friends.

Today I would like to speak about Dussehra, a festival that fills every street with color, music and the smell of hot jalebis.

Dussehra, also called Vijayadashami, falls on the tenth day after Navratri begins. In the Ramayana, Lord Rama fought the mighty king Ravana to rescue Sita, and won. In eastern India, people celebrate Goddess Durga's victory over the demon Mahishasura on the same day. Both stories carry one message: however strong evil looks, good wins in the end.

Our country celebrates this day in many beautiful ways. In the north we watch Ramlila and see giant effigies of Ravana burn. In Kolkata, Durga Puja ends with immersion processions. Mysuru has a royal elephant procession, Maharashtra exchanges Apta leaves as gold, and in Kerala little children write their very first letters.

Friends, Ravana was brave and learned, but pride destroyed him. Each of us has a small Ravana inside: maybe anger, maybe laziness, maybe the habit of copying homework. This Dussehra, let us each choose one bad habit and defeat it.

Let us also celebrate safely, keep away from fire and fireworks, and keep our surroundings clean.

Happy Dussehra to everyone. Thank you!

Explaining Dussehra to children

A child-friendly explanation can begin with a simple idea: Dussehra is a festival about courage, good choices, learning and the victory of what is right over what is harmful. Then add regional context. Some families tell the story of Rama and Ravana, some focus on Goddess Durga and Mahishasura, and some emphasize Ayudha Puja, Vidyarambham or other traditions.

Use language appropriate to the student's age and avoid presenting mythology as if every Indian family interprets it identically.

Simple facts for students

  • Dussehra is also called Dasara or Vijayadashami in many regions.
  • It is connected with the conclusion of Navratri.
  • Ramayana traditions are especially prominent in many north Indian celebrations.
  • Durga Puja and Vijayadashami are especially important in eastern India.
  • Ayudha Puja and Vidyarambham are important in several southern traditions.
  • The festival can include puja, processions, Ramlila, fairs, family visits, food and learning rituals.

School project structure

A strong school project can use five sections:

  1. What is Dussehra?
  2. Why is it celebrated?
  3. How do different regions celebrate?
  4. What values are associated with the festival?
  5. What did the student learn?

Add a map, a simple timeline, labeled drawings or a small glossary rather than filling the page with copied text.

Craft and activity safety

Use paper, cardboard, crayons, washable colors and child-safe scissors. Adults should handle cutting tools, hot glue, fire, fireworks and any activity involving large effigies. For school projects, a paper model of Ravana is safer and more practical than anything intended to burn.

Short paragraph for younger students

Dussehra is an Indian festival celebrated in many different ways. In many places, people remember Lord Rama's victory over Ravana. In other traditions, Vijayadashami celebrates Goddess Durga's victory over Mahishasura. Families may pray, visit fairs, watch Ramlila, share food, honor books or tools, and begin something new. The festival teaches values such as courage, discipline and respect.

Common mistakes to avoid

A frequent mistake is presenting one regional custom as if it represents all of India. Another is copying ritual instructions without knowing whether they belong to the family's own tradition.

Accuracy improves when you distinguish scripture, later retellings, folklore, local custom and modern festival practice. For public events, never trade safety for a better view or photograph.

Frequently asked questions

Is Dussehra celebrated the same way everywhere?

No. Dussehra and Vijayadashami traditions vary substantially by region, language, family and religious tradition.

Is Dussehra the same as Vijayadashami?

The terms often refer to the same festival day, but regional usage and the traditions emphasized can differ.

Does every Dussehra celebration include Ravana Dahan?

No. Ravana Dahan is prominent in many north Indian celebrations, but many communities observe Vijayadashami without burning an effigy.

How can families keep the celebration simple?

Choose the customs that are meaningful to your household: a short puja, blessings from elders, a festive meal, reading or storytelling, or attending one community event.

What is the best way to learn the local tradition?

Ask family elders or community organizers and consult reputable temple, cultural or regional sources rather than assuming a generic checklist applies everywhere.

Final thoughts

Like every part of Dussehra and Vijayadashami, this is best understood in the festival’s wider context rather than as an isolated custom. The festival's strength lies in its diversity: families and communities can honor inherited traditions while still adapting celebration details to modern life, safety needs and local circumstances.
