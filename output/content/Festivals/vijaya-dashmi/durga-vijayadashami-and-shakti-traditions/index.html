Durga & Vijayadashami

Durga’s victory, the last day of Durga Puja, sindoor khela, visarjan and Bijoya in eastern India.

20 guides by Prashant Dhingra

  • Vijayadashami and Goddess Durga: Story, Meaning and Traditions In Shakta tradition Vijayadashami marks Durga's victory over Mahishasura after nine nights of battle, and the end of Navratri and Durga Puja.
  • Mahishasura Mardini: The Story Behind Durga’s Victory Mahishasura Mardini means 'slayer of Mahishasura': Durga, formed from the combined energy of the gods, defeats the buffalo demon whom no god or man could kill.
  • Why Vijayadashami Marks the End of Durga Puja Vijayadashami ends Durga Puja because it is the day of Durga's victory and, in Bengali belief, the day she leaves her parents' home to return to Shiva on Kailash.
  • Durga Puja Visarjan and Vijayadashami: What Happens on the Final Day On Vijayadashami the priest performs the dashami puja and darpan visarjan, symbolically immersing the goddess's reflection in a mirror.
  • Sindoor Khela on Vijayadashami: Meaning and Tradition Sindoor khela is the Bengali Vijayadashami ritual where married women offer vermilion to Durga, then smear it on each other's foreheads and cheeks before the idol is immersed.
  • Bijoya Dashami: Bengali Traditions, Greetings and Customs Bijoya Dashami is the Bengali name for Vijayadashami, when Durga's idols are immersed and people say 'Shubho Bijoya'.
  • Why Durga Is Seen as a Symbol of Shakti on Vijayadashami Durga embodies Shakti, the active divine power, because the Devi Mahatmya describes her as formed from the combined energies of all the gods to do what none could do alone.
  • Navratri to Vijayadashami: How the Ten-Day Festival Cycle Works Navratri runs nine nights from Ashvin Shukla Pratipada, often grouped as three nights each for Durga, Lakshmi and Saraswati or one for each Navadurga form.
  • Dussehra and Durga Puja: Similarities and Differences Dussehra and Durga Puja meet on the same day, Vijayadashami, but stress different stories.
  • Vijayadashami Traditions in West Bengal In West Bengal Vijayadashami is Bijoya Dashami, the last day of Durga Puja.
  • Vijayadashami Traditions in Assam In Assam, Vijayadashami ends a Durga Puja season that fills Guwahati and towns across the state with pandals.
  • Vijayadashami Traditions in Odisha In Odisha, Vijayadashami (Dasahara) ends Durga Puja, with visarjan processions and, in some towns, Ravana Podi (effigy burning).
  • Vijayadashami Traditions in Tripura In Tripura, Vijayadashami ends Durga Puja, which is the state's biggest festival.
  • How Families Celebrate Bijoya Dashami at Home At home, families mark Bijoya Dashami by watching or joining the immersion and touching elders' feet.
  • Traditional Foods Served on Bijoya Dashami Typical Bijoya Dashami foods are narkel naru (coconut laddoo), nimki (savory crackers), ghugni (dried pea curry), mishti such as rosogolla and sandesh, and payesh.
  • Vijayadashami Blessings and Respect for Elders Seeking elders' blessings is one of the most widely shared Vijayadashami customs: in Bengal through pranam, in Maharashtra by offering Apta leaves, and in Nepal's Dashain by receiving tika and jamara.
  • Why People Visit Friends and Family on Bijoya Dashami People visit on Bijoya Dashami to share the goddess's farewell, renew ties and take or give blessings.
  • Durga Puja Pandals to Visarjan: The Journey to Vijayadashami Durga Puja moves from Mahalaya and the idol-makers of Kumartuli to pandal openings on Shashthi, Saptami and Ashtami rituals, Sandhi Puja and Navami.
  • The Spiritual Meaning of Durga’s Return on Vijayadashami Spiritually, Durga's return on Vijayadashami means the divine mother, having destroyed what harms, goes home and leaves her strength with her devotees.
  • Vijayadashami for Beginners: A Guide to Bengali and Shakti Traditions For beginners, Vijayadashami in Bengali and Shakta tradition is the last day of Durga Puja: the goddess has won, and families bid her farewell.
