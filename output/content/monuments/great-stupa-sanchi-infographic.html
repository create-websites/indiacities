A MONUMENT OF DYNASTIES

The stupa we see today took shape over time, evolving as each great dynasty contributed to its design and grandeur.

MAURYA DYNASTY (c. 3rd Century BCE)

The Foundation

Emperor Ashoka establishes the base, crafting a modest brick dome for Buddha's relics and raising his famed pillar.

SHUNGA DYNASTY (c. 2nd Century BCE)

The Expansion

The stupa was enlarged, covered in stone, and surrounded by majestic stone railings (*vedikas*).

SATAVAHANA DYNASTY (c. 1st Century BCE)

The Embellishment

Four splendidly crafted gateways (*toranas*) stand adorned, illustrating tales from the Buddha's journey.

GUPTA DYNASTY (c. 5th Century CE)

The Evolution

Signaling a change in devotion, four Buddha-image shrines are placed at the stupa's gateways.

ANATOMY OF A STUPA

Every section of the Great Stupa forms part of a symbolic cosmic design.

  • 1.
    Chhatra: The threefold umbrella symbolizes Buddhism's Three Jewels: Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha.
  • 2.
    Harmika: The square railing represents the dwelling of the divine, a holy realm distinct from the mortal sphere.
  • 3.
    Anda: The grand dome, symbolizing the boundless heavens and housing the revered relics inside.

A conceptual depiction of the pivotal dynastic influences shaping the stupa's enduring legacy through time.

THE LANGUAGE OF SYMBOLS

In Sanchi's ancient art, the Buddha was symbolized, not depicted, with his essence shown through carved gateway icons.
