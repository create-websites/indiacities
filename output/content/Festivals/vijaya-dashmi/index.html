What is Dussehra?

Dussehra, also called Vijayadashami, falls on the tenth day of the bright half of Ashvin, right after the nine nights of Navratri. In the north it recalls Rama's victory over Ravana; in the east it closes Durga Puja; in the south it carries Ayudha Puja and Vidyarambham. Ramlila, effigy burning, Shami worship, blessings from elders and festive food are the most common ways people mark it.

Six places, six ways to celebrate

The same day looks completely different in Mysuru, Kullu, Varanasi, Bastar, Delhi and Ayodhya.

  • Mysuru Dussehra in Mysuru: Royal Procession, Palace Lights and Traditions Mysuru Dasara is Karnataka's state festival (Nadahabba): ten days of cultural events, with Mysore Palace lit by nearly 100,000 bulbs each evening.
  • Kullu Kullu Dussehra: History, Deities and Week-Long Celebrations Kullu Dussehra begins on Vijayadashami, as celebrations elsewhere end, and runs seven days at Dhalpur Maidan.
  • Varanasi Dussehra in Varanasi: Ramnagar Ramlila and Local Traditions Varanasi's Dussehra centers on the Ramnagar Ramlila, a month-long promenade performance across the town opposite the ghats, patronized by the Kashi royal family since the 19th century.
  • Bastar Dussehra in Bastar: Tribal Traditions and Cultural Guide Bastar Dussehra in Chhattisgarh lasts 75 days, among India's longest festivals, and honors the goddess Danteshwari rather than Rama.
  • Delhi Dussehra in Delhi: Ramlila, Effigy Burning and Community Celebrations Delhi celebrates Dussehra with some of India's biggest Ramlilas, at Ramlila Maidan, the Red Fort grounds and hundreds of neighborhood stages, ending with towering Ravana effigies burned at dusk.
  • Ayodhya Dussehra in Ayodhya: Rama Traditions and Festival Celebrations In Ayodhya, revered as Rama's birthplace, Dussehra brings Ramlila, temple worship, processions and effigy burning along the Saryu.

Quick answers

When is Dussehra?

Dussehra falls on Ashvin Shukla Dashami, the tenth day after Navratri begins, usually in September or October. Most calendars place Dussehra 2027 on Saturday, 9 October; a few panchangs for Indian cities list 10 October.

Is Dussehra the same as Vijayadashami?

Yes. They are two names for the same day. Karnataka and Maharashtra say Dasara, Bengal says Bijoya Dashami and Nepal celebrates Dashain.

Why is Dussehra celebrated?

It celebrates Rama’s victory over Ravana in the Ramayana and Durga’s victory over Mahishasura in Shakta tradition, the victory of good over evil.

Does every region burn a Ravana effigy?

No. Ravana Dahan is mainly a north Indian custom. Mysuru, Kullu, Bastar, Bengal, Kerala and most of the south mark the day in other ways.

Is Vijayadashami good for new beginnings?

Yes. The whole day is traditionally considered auspicious, so people start studies, businesses and purchases, and children begin writing in Vidyarambham.
