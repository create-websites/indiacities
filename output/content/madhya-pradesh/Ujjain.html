Ujjain: The Greenwich of India

A city with a rich history nestled along the banks of the revered Shipra River, where time and spirituality intertwine.

Overview

Ujjain, located in western Madhya Pradesh, is renowned as a significant holy city in India. Formerly known as Ujjayini, it is often called the 'Greenwich of India' due to its historical importance. The city was a hub for mathematical and astronomical studies, housing renowned scholars such as Brahmagupta and Varahamihira.

Altitude: 212 meters above sea level
Annual Rainfall: 152 cms
Best Time: October to March
Languages: Hindi, English

History & Heritage

Ujjain has a rich history, being the former residence of Ashoka during his time as viceroy of the Maurya Empire's western provinces. It is also known as the traditional capital of King Vikramaditya, whose court was home to the 'nine jewels' of Sanskrit literature, such as the renowned poet Kalidas.

Ujjain served as the capital of the Maratha leader Sindhia before being incorporated into the Gwalior state in the late 18th century. After Indian independence, Ujjain became a part of Madhya Pradesh in 1956.

Sacred Attractions

Maha Kumbh Mela

Every twelve years, pilgrims and sadhus flock to the banks of the sacred Shipra River for the grand bathing rituals.

Mahakaleshwar Temple

Devoted to Lord Shiva, this temple contains one of the 12 revered Jyotirlingas. The building is split into five tiers, with a lower level illuminated by brass lamps.

Bade Ganeshji Ka Mandir

One of India's largest Lord Ganesha statues can be found here, along with a distinctive Panchmukhi image of Lord Hanuman.

Bhartrihari Caves

Named in honor of Bhartrihari, the scholar-poet and step-brother of King Vikramaditya, who sought solace in meditation at this

Cultural Landmarks

  • Vikram Kirti Mandir: A cultural hub showcasing the Scindia Oriental Research Institute, boasting a collection of more than 18,000 valuable manuscripts.
  • Kailadeh Palace: Constructed by Mandu rulers in 1458 on an island in the Shipra river with Persian architectural influence.

Travel Guide

By Air

The nearest airport is at Indore (55 km), with connections to Delhi, Mumbai, and Bhopal.

By Rail

A crucial stop on the Western Railway network, with connections to numerous major cities across India.

By Road

Linked by regular state transport buses to nearly every city in Madhya Pradesh.
