Patna: The Ancient Pataliputra

A collection of ancient knowledge and wisdom stretching back to 600 B.C.

Quick Facts

Area: 107.08 sq. km
Altitude: 53m above sea level
Best Time: October to March
Climate: 5°C to 43°C
Languages: Hindi, English
STD Code: 0612

Imperial History

Patna, originally known as Pataliputra, was the capital of Chandragupta MauryaAfter the fall of the Mauryan Empire, it experienced a resurgence under the rule of an Afghan leader. Sher Shah Suri in the 16th century.

The city was home to influential figures such as Emperor Ashoka and the Gupta dynasty, as well as esteemed scholars like Kautilya This place was where they created their masterpieces, and renowned Chinese explorers Fa-Hien and Hiuen-Tsang came to record its knowledge.

Governor Azim-us-Shan oversaw the transformation of the city into the beautiful town of 'Azimabad,' which later became a significant silk and calico trade center under British rule.

Major Attractions

Mahatma Gandhi Setu

One of the world's longest bridges, the structure spans 5450 meters and links north and south Bihar via forty piers.

Sher Shah Masjid

The largest mosque in Patna, constructed in Afghan style under the reign of Sher Shah Suri, features a distinctive design where only three of its domes can be seen from any viewpoint.

Sanjay Gandhi Biological Park

Home to more than 300 species of flora, including rare orchids, ferns, and roses, India's largest zoo is a sight to behold.

Spiritual Heritage

Phulwari Sharif

A hub of Sufi influence dating back to medieval times, featuring notable structures such as Khanqah Mujeebia and the Shahi Sangi Masjid, constructed by Humayun with red sandstone.

Hajipur (Ancient Ukkacala)

This location is renowned as the spot where Lord Buddha delivered his teachings and where Ananda, his most trusted disciple, is said to have died in a fiery blaze.
