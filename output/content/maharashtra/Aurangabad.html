Aurangabad City Trip

A gateway to ancient landmarks and a vibrant display of Mughal culture.

City Overview

Aurangabad is known for its stunning hill surroundings and rich cultural heritage, featuring medieval monuments from the Mughal era. The city is also recognized for its thriving silk and cotton textile industries.

State: Maharashtra
Altitude: 513 meters
Best Time: Sept to March
Marathi, Hindi, Urdu

History

In 1610 A.D., Malik Amber established the city on the grounds of Khirki village. It was initially known as Fatehnagar until Prince Aurangzeb declared it his capital in 1633, renaming it Aurangabad.

In the past, the area was located along an old trade path that linked Ujjaini in the north with the Satwahana capital, Pratishthana, in the south.

Aurangabad Caves

There are twelve Buddhist caves, dating from the 1st to the 7th century A.D., with walls adorned with Jataka story illustrations influenced by Tantric Hinduism.

Panchakki

A water mill created to crush grains for pilgrims, utilizing water power from a mountain spring channeled through hidden pipes.

Ellora Caves

30 kilometers away from the city are the 7th-century caves showcasing the Kailashnath Temple, a stunning example of Dravidian architecture.

Nearby Excursions

  • Daulatabad Fort

    Constructed in the 12th Century A.D. under the name Devgiri, it is situated 13 km away from Aurangabad.

  • Shirdi

    A renowned religious destination showcasing the Shrine of Sai Baba, situated 144 kilometers away from Aurangabad.

  • Ajanta Caves

    A UNESCO World Heritage site, consisting of 29 caves filled with Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain artwork dating back to the 2nd century B.C.

By Road
