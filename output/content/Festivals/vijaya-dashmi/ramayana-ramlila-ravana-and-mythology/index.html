Ramayana & Ramlila

Rama, Sita, Hanuman and Ravana, the Ramlila tradition, and how the epic is read and taught today.

20 guides by Prashant Dhingra

  • Rama vs Ravana: The Ramayana Story Behind Dussehra Rama is the prince of Ayodhya exiled for fourteen years; Ravana is the learned but proud king of Lanka who abducts Rama's wife Sita.
  • Who Was Ravana? Mythology, Scholarship and Popular Interpretations Ravana was the ten-headed king of Lanka in the Ramayana, son of the sage Vishrava, brother of Kubera, a great devotee of Shiva and a famed scholar and musician.
  • Why Did Lord Rama Defeat Ravana? In the Ramayana, Rama defeats Ravana because Ravana abducted Sita and refused every chance, including Vibhishana's counsel, to return her.
  • The Role of Sita in the Ramayana and Dussehra Traditions Sita is the Ramayana's moral center: Rama's wife, who chooses exile with him, resists Ravana throughout her captivity in Lanka's Ashoka grove, and whose rescue is the reason for the war.
  • Lakshmana’s Role in the Ramayana and Dussehra Story Lakshmana is Rama's younger brother, who leaves Ayodhya to share his fourteen-year exile and guards Rama and Sita throughout.
  • Hanuman’s Role in Rama’s Victory Over Ravana Hanuman finds Sita in Lanka, delivers Rama's ring and sets the city alight with his burning tail.
  • Vibhishana: Why Ravana’s Brother Supported Rama Vibhishana, Ravana's younger brother, urged him to return Sita.
  • Kumbhakarna: The Story Behind Ravana’s Powerful Brother Kumbhakarna is Ravana's giant brother, who asked Brahma for a boon and, through a slip of the tongue, received long sleep instead.
  • Meghnad or Indrajit: The Warrior Son of Ravana Meghnad, also called Indrajit ('conqueror of Indra'), is Ravana's eldest son, a master of illusion who fought invisibly from the clouds.
  • Why Are Ravana, Kumbhakarna and Meghnad Effigies Burned Together? Ravana, his brother Kumbhakarna and his son Meghnad are burned together because they were the three great warriors of Lanka whom Rama's side defeated in the final war.
  • Ramlila: History, Meaning and How It Is Performed Ramlila ('Rama's play') is a folk retelling of the Ramayana, staged over the nights of Navratri and ending with Ravana's defeat on Dussehra.
  • Famous Ramlila Traditions of North India North India's best-known Ramlilas include Ramnagar in Varanasi, a month-long promenade play under the patronage of the Kashi royal family, and Delhi's large Ramlilas at Ramlila Maidan and the Red Fort grounds.
  • How Ramlila Retells the Ramayana for Modern Audiences Modern Ramlilas keep the Ramcharitmanas storyline but use LED stages, sound systems, film-style costumes, live streams and shorter runs.
  • Ravana in Different Ramayana Traditions Across India Ravana is drawn differently across Ramayana traditions.
  • Lessons from the Ramayana Commonly Associated with Dussehra The lessons most often linked to Dussehra are that pride destroys (Ravana), loyalty sustains (Lakshmana, Hanuman), courage means standing by what is right even against family (Vibhishana), and dignity survives captivity (Sita).
  • Ten Heads of Ravana: What Do They Symbolize? Ravana's ten heads are explained literally as a boon and symbolically as mastery of the four Vedas and six Shastras.
  • Was Ravana Only a Villain? Understanding Different Cultural Perspectives Ravana is the Ramayana's antagonist, but many traditions also remember him as a scholar, a musician remembered in the name of the Ravanahatha fiddle, a devotee of Shiva and a capable king.
  • Dussehra and the Idea of Dharma in the Ramayana In the Ramayana, dharma means right conduct in one's role: Rama keeps his father's word and goes into exile, fights to rescue Sita and accepts Vibhishana's surrender.
  • How the Ramayana Is Taught to Children During Dussehra During Dussehra children learn the Ramayana through Ramlila, picture books, animated films, storytelling by grandparents, school plays and craft projects such as paper effigies.
  • Ramayana Characters Guide for Dussehra: Rama, Sita, Hanuman, Ravana and More The Ramayana's main characters are Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, Hanuman, Ravana, Vibhishana, Kumbhakarna, Meghnad, Bharata, Dasharatha, Kaikeyi, Sugriva, Jatayu and Shurpanakha.
