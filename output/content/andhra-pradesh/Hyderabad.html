History of Hyderabad

Hyderabad's history began with the establishment of the Qutub Shahi dynasty In 1512, the fortress city of Golconda was the starting point for a redesign of the city. Charminar to combat plague and cholera.

the city of Golconda witnessed the ascent of power after Aurangazeb's conquest in 1687. Asaf Jah-I, declaring himself the Nizam, ruled until 1948.

The twin city of Secunderabad Established in 1798 as a British cantonment and named after Nizam Sikander Jah, the two cities have now combined to form a vast metropolitan area.

The Pearl Culture

Despite being far from the coast, Hyderabad is synonymous with luminescent pearlsThe historical royal patronage from the Arabian Gulf attracted craftsmen, leading to this outcome.

Visit Patther Gatti, a street filled with pearl shops, showcasing the dazzling heritage of the Nizams.

Iconic Sightseeing

Golconda Fort

This impressive ruin was once home to the renowned Kohinoor diamond. The fort's walls are made of massive masonry blocks, some weighing up to 7 tons, and span nearly 7km in circumference.

Charminar

Constructed in 1591 to mark the conclusion of a devastating epidemic, the city's main landmark boasts four illuminated arches that point towards the cardinal directions.

Salar Jung Museum

Salar Jung III amassed one of the world's largest private collections, boasting over 35,000 items such as Persian miniatures and weaponry.

Hussain Sagar

Tank Bund, a vast lake showcasing the world's heaviest stone Buddha statue, weighing 350 tons, has transformed into a popular destination for water sports and leisure activities.

Historic & Spiritual Sites

Mecca Masjid

This mosque, one of the world's largest, is located next to Charminar. It gets its name from bricks made of soil imported from Mecca.

Qutub Shahi & Paigah Tombs

The Qutub Shahi kings' cemetery displays elegant domed tombs, while the Paigah tombs exhibit intricate Persian and Rajasthani inlay designs.

Osmania University

Established in 1919, the Arts College building is a stunning fusion of European and Indian architectural designs.
